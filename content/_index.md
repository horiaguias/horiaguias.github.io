## Job Market Paper

### "Perceived Responsibility for Negative Externalities"
[Download Paper (PDF)](jmp.pdf) | [oTree Experimental Code](https://github.com/horiaguias/Responsibility-Functions-Experimental-Code)

**Abstract:** Why do individuals pay to offset their own emissions but not the same emissions caused by someone else? I study to what extent perceived responsibility for negative externalities is shaped by past-caused harm versus anticipated consequences and analyze its effect on decision making. I develop a stylized theoretical framework in which a decision maker can repair self-caused harm and exogenous background harm. Beyond outcome-based preferences, she cares about maintaining a moral self-image that is activated by backward-looking responsibility. I provide conditions under which the decision maker does not find it efficient or equitable to repair harm yet exclusively repairs self-caused harm to restore her self-image, thus ignoring background harm for which she does not feel responsible. Furthermore, I develop a sharp revealed preference test showing that under a responsibility-driven moral self-image, a decision maker responds to marginal changes in the causal history of harm – a behavior outcome-based models of other-regarding preferences cannot rationalize. To test this, I design a laboratory experiment in which I isolate causal history from anticipated consequences. Moreover, I vary the price to repair self-caused and existing harm. By structurally estimating preferences, I find considerable heterogeneity in the influence of backward-looking responsibility on behavior. Results at the aggregate level reveal subjects exhibit a significant responsibility premium of 29%-43% when paying to repair self-caused harm. These results provide a foundation to study the limits of individual responsibility in consumer markets, the demand for voluntary carbon offsets, and the efficiency of CSR activities.
